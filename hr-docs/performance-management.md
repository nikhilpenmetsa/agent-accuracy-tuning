# ACME Corporation Performance Management

## Performance Management Philosophy

At ACME, we believe in continuous feedback and development. Our performance management process is designed to:
- Align individual goals with company objectives
- Provide regular feedback and coaching
- Recognize and reward strong performance
- Identify development opportunities
- Support career growth

## Performance Review Cycle

### Annual Review Timeline

**January-February:** Goal Setting
- Employees and managers collaborate on annual goals
- Goals aligned with department and company objectives
- Documented in Workday

**June:** Mid-Year Review
- Informal check-in on goal progress
- Adjust goals if needed based on business changes
- Discuss development needs

**November:** Self-Assessment
- Employees complete self-assessment in Workday
- Reflect on accomplishments and challenges
- Due by November 15

**December:** Annual Performance Review
- Manager completes performance evaluation
- Review meeting scheduled with employee
- Performance rating assigned
- Merit increase determined (effective January 1)

## Goal Setting (SMART Goals)

All goals should be SMART:
- **Specific:** Clear and well-defined
- **Measurable:** Quantifiable metrics for success
- **Achievable:** Realistic given resources and constraints
- **Relevant:** Aligned with role and company objectives
- **Time-bound:** Clear deadline or timeframe

### Example Goals

**Sales Role:**
"Increase quarterly sales revenue by 15% (from $500K to $575K) by December 31 through expanding existing accounts and acquiring 10 new customers."

**Engineering Role:**
"Reduce application load time by 30% (from 3 seconds to 2 seconds) by Q3 through code optimization and implementing caching strategies."

**HR Role:**
"Improve employee engagement score from 72% to 80% by year-end through implementing quarterly pulse surveys and addressing top 3 feedback themes."

### Goal Categories

**Business Goals (60-70% of goals)**
- Directly tied to job responsibilities
- Measurable business outcomes
- Aligned with department objectives

**Development Goals (20-30% of goals)**
- Skill building and learning
- Career growth activities
- Leadership development

**Company Values Goals (10-20% of goals)**
- Demonstrate company values
- Collaboration and teamwork
- Innovation and continuous improvement

## Performance Ratings

### Rating Scale

**5 - Exceptional (Top 5%)**
- Consistently exceeds expectations in all areas
- Delivers extraordinary results
- Demonstrates exceptional leadership
- Significant impact beyond role scope

**4 - Exceeds Expectations (15-20%)**
- Regularly exceeds performance expectations
- Achieves all goals and stretches beyond
- Strong contributor to team success
- Role model for company values

**3 - Meets Expectations (60-70%)**
- Consistently meets performance expectations
- Achieves most or all goals
- Reliable and effective contributor
- Demonstrates company values

**2 - Needs Improvement (5-10%)**
- Performance below expectations in some areas
- Did not achieve several key goals
- Requires additional support and coaching
- Improvement plan required

**1 - Unsatisfactory (Rare)**
- Performance significantly below expectations
- Failed to achieve most goals
- Immediate improvement required
- May result in termination if not improved

### Rating Distribution

ACME uses a calibration process to ensure fair and consistent ratings across the organization. Managers meet to discuss ratings and ensure:
- Consistency in rating standards
- Fair distribution across teams
- Recognition of top performers
- Support for those needing improvement

## Continuous Feedback

### Regular 1:1 Meetings
- Frequency: Weekly or bi-weekly
- Duration: 30-60 minutes
- Topics: Progress updates, challenges, feedback, development

### Real-Time Feedback
Managers should provide feedback:
- Immediately after significant accomplishments
- When coaching opportunities arise
- Throughout the year, not just at review time

### 360-Degree Feedback
For leadership roles and high-potential employees:
- Feedback from manager, peers, direct reports
- Anonymous and confidential
- Used for development, not performance rating
- Conducted annually or bi-annually

## Merit Increases and Bonuses

### Merit Increase Guidelines
Based on performance rating and position in salary range:

**Rating 5 (Exceptional):**
- Merit increase: 5-8%
- Bonus: 15-25% of base salary

**Rating 4 (Exceeds):**
- Merit increase: 3-5%
- Bonus: 10-15% of base salary

**Rating 3 (Meets):**
- Merit increase: 2-3%
- Bonus: 5-10% of base salary

**Rating 2 (Needs Improvement):**
- Merit increase: 0-1%
- No bonus

**Rating 1 (Unsatisfactory):**
- No merit increase
- No bonus
- Performance improvement plan

### Bonus Eligibility
- Must be employed as of December 31
- Must not be on performance improvement plan
- Prorated for employees hired after April 1
- Paid in March following review year

## Performance Improvement Plans (PIP)

### When PIPs Are Used
- Performance rating of 2 or 1
- Consistent failure to meet expectations
- Specific performance or behavioral issues
- As alternative to immediate termination

### PIP Process

**Step 1: PIP Meeting**
- Manager and HR meet with employee
- Discuss performance concerns
- Review specific examples and expectations
- Present written PIP document

**Step 2: PIP Period (30-90 days)**
- Clear performance goals and metrics
- Regular check-ins (weekly)
- Support and resources provided
- Progress documented

**Step 3: PIP Conclusion**
- Final evaluation meeting
- Outcomes:
  - **Successful:** Performance improved, PIP closed
  - **Partially Successful:** PIP extended
  - **Unsuccessful:** Employment terminated

### Employee Rights During PIP
- Right to respond to performance concerns
- Access to HR support
- Opportunity to improve with clear expectations
- Regular feedback on progress

## Promotion Process

### Eligibility
- Minimum 12 months in current role
- Meets expectations or exceeds in current role
- Demonstrates readiness for next level
- Open position available or role expansion justified

### Promotion Criteria
- Consistently performs at next level
- Possesses required skills and competencies
- Demonstrates leadership potential
- Supports team and company success

### Promotion Timeline
- Promotions typically effective January 1 or July 1
- Discussed during performance review cycle
- Requires manager recommendation and skip-level approval
- HR reviews for equity and consistency

## Performance Management FAQs

**Q: What if I disagree with my performance rating?**
A: Discuss concerns with your manager first. If unresolved, contact your HR Business Partner or use the formal appeals process.

**Q: Can my goals change during the year?**
A: Yes, goals can be adjusted based on business needs or role changes. Discuss with your manager and update in Workday.

**Q: How is my rating determined?**
A: Based on achievement of goals, demonstration of competencies, and overall impact. Your manager considers your performance throughout the year.

**Q: What if I'm on leave during review time?**
A: Reviews are prorated based on time worked. Discuss timing with your manager and HR.

**Q: Can I see my previous performance reviews?**
A: Yes, all reviews are stored in Workday under Performance > My Reviews.

**Q: What if I'm new and haven't been here a full year?**
A: New hires receive a prorated review if employed for at least 6 months by review date. Otherwise, you'll participate in the next cycle.

**Q: How do I prepare for my performance review?**
A: Complete your self-assessment thoughtfully, gather examples of accomplishments, review your goals, and prepare questions for your manager.

## Manager Responsibilities

### Throughout the Year
- Set clear expectations and goals
- Provide regular feedback and coaching
- Document performance (good and bad)
- Support employee development
- Address performance issues promptly

### During Review Cycle
- Complete performance evaluations on time
- Provide specific examples and evidence
- Conduct thoughtful review conversations
- Recommend merit increases and bonuses
- Participate in calibration sessions

### Best Practices
- No surprises - feedback should be ongoing
- Be specific and objective
- Focus on behaviors and results, not personality
- Balance positive feedback with development areas
- Listen to employee perspective
